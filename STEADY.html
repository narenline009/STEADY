Comparative Optics Analysis

Optical Stabilization (OIS) vs. Electronic Cropping (EIS)

All three frames below experience the exact same simulated hand shake. Notice how physical OIS preserves the outer green corner markers without motion smear, while software EIS crops into the image and cannot remove smear created during exposure.

EIS Digital Crop Margin 20%
1. NO STABILIZATION 100% Field of View

Full sensor area is recorded, but camera rotation smears light across pixels during exposure and shakes the frame.

2. OPTICAL (OIS) ~100% Field of View

Physical lens shift keeps light rays stationary on the sensor during exposure—sharp detail with full wide edges intact.

3. ELECTRONIC (EIS) 80% Field of View

Software shifts a smaller crop window (dashed rectangle) between frames, sacrificing outer edges and retaining exposure blur.

Rolling Shutter vs. Global Shutter

Most smartphone CMOS sensors read pixel rows sequentially from top to bottom rather than all at once. When a propeller spins rapidly while the readout scan sweeps downward, straight blades appear curved.

Sensor Readout Time 25 ms
Propeller Speed Fast
Sequential Row Readout

On the left is the actual spinning rotor with the horizontal readout scan line sweeping downward. On the right is the resulting sensor image assembled row by row from successive instants in time.
